2. Freeze forecast

1. Native float atomics for PME spreading on Apple9 (pme, 71a602b43, +4 lines). Expected single: pme 1.09 to 1.13, amber20-dhfr 1.09 to 1.13, apoa1pme 1.14 to 1.18, apoa1ljpme 1.16 to 1.20, cellulose 1.10 to 1.15, stmv 1.12 to 1.18. Mixed gains the same microseconds on longer steps: pme 1.06 to 1.09, apoa1pme 1.12 to 1.15. The M2 is unchanged by construction. The main risk is the gate's third-digit rel|dF| comparison flipping, because the spread is now order-dependent. There is no real physics risk: CUDA ships the same path.

2. SIMD-group-per-element sortShortList2, plus copyShortList (nblist, split out of 9c253281c). Expected single: rf 1.06 to 1.09, pme 1.05 to 1.07, dhfr 1.05 to 1.075, gbsa 1.01 to 1.025. Mixed about 0.7x those. The apoa1 tests and larger do not use this path. The main risk is that the kernel has never run: Metal compiles it at context creation, and it is the first kernel with [[thread_index_in_simdgroup]] parameters. The second risk is that the 48.5 us is partly the blit encoder switch rather than kernel work.

3. PME and block sort cost: multi-threadgroup computeRange (nblist) stacked with nosort (pme, 17929e631). Expected combined single: apoa1ljpme 1.08 to 1.12, apoa1pme 1.03 to 1.05, cellulose 1.04 to 1.06, stmv 1.04 to 1.07, pme and dhfr 1.02 to 1.04, apoa1rf about 1.01. nosort already screened at pme 1.034, apoa1pme 1.022, and M2 apoa1ljpme 1.033. The main risk is double counting: nosort deletes the PME sorts that computeRange speeds up, so each must be measured on top of the other. The other risk is the bimodal slow mode from a bad merged range, which only shows across fresh processes.

4. Tiles-per-batch in findBlocksWithInteractions, as a device-scaled rule (owned by nblist). Expected single: rf 1.05 to 1.10, pme and dhfr 1.03 to 1.07, gbsa 0.99 to 1.03, apoa1 and larger 1.00 (the compiled kernel is identical). Lab 024 saw 9 of 9 variants win on rf and pme, and the M2 was flat. The main risk is extra partial tiles inflating computeNonbonded every step, while the saving only lands on the 50 to 75% of steps that rebuild. gbsa and the M2 need the quiet window.

5. Mixed constraint bundle c1+c3+c4, with c2 limited to div and sqrt (mixed, Metal-gated). Expected mixed only: pme, rf and dhfr 1.08 to 1.12, gbsa 1.04 to 1.07, apoa1rf and apoa1pme 1.03 to 1.05, apoa1ljpme and cellulose 1.01 to 1.03. Single is unaffected. The main risk is accuracy. The gate never runs constraints, and the ctest tolerances are loose enough for single. Only drift.py and constraints.py can reject it, and neither has produced a result yet.

6. Force words in the nonbonded kernel (atomics). This is the high-variance entry. Expected single, on top of float atomics: apoa1rf 1.07 to 1.15, rf 1.04 to 1.08, apoa1pme 1.03 to 1.08, cellulose and stmv 1.03 to 1.08, pme and dhfr 1.02 to 1.05, gbsa 0.99 to 1.01. The main risks are that the returning atomic and the 2 to 3 plane address scatter eat most of the op-count saving, and that the fold dispatch pushes gbsa past -1%. It has not been timed at all, so it may miss the freeze.

The poll-based event wait (dispatch) is a dark horse: gbsa about 1.03 to 1.035, rf 1.01 to 1.03, everything else flat. It is right at the 3% line and cheap to try.

3. Gaps

- The findBlocksWithInteractions kernel body and the rebuild rate. findBlocks is 30% of rf, 22% of pme, 25% of apoa1rf, 20% of apoa1pme, 16% of cellulose and 13% of stmv. Rebuilds happen on 50 to 75% of steps. The only work touching it is launch shape (tiles, NB_FBG). Per-rebuild cost scales badly with size (0.38 us per block on dhfr against 0.06 on stmv), and nobody is looking at the rebuild trigger, the padding tradeoff with data, or the kernel's own inner loop. nblist should own this once sort2 and range are in.
- computeBondedForces. It is 16% of apoa1rf (185 us), 14% of apoa1pme (254 us), 15% of cellulose (955 us), 11% of stmv and apoa1ljpme, and 6 to 7% of pme and dhfr. It runs as one merged kernel, and nobody owns it. The atomics lane should take it, starting with the one-atomic bonded probe to find out whether it is atomic-bound at all.
- GBSA OBC kernels. computeBornSum (45 us) plus computeGBSAForce1 (52 us) is 33% of the gbsa step. gbsa is the weakest mixed test (0.715) and the one every candidate risks losing on. Nobody owns it. It uses the nonbonded tile pattern, so the nonbonded lane should take it once the TG-variant family is settled.
- The PME second queue. It is off by default (MetalDisablePmeStream "true"), so reciprocal space runs strictly after the direct-space work. The pme lane measured about 1.0 ms reciprocal plus 1.1 ms direct, with no overlap, on apoa1pme. Nobody is testing the property flip. pme owns it. Coordinate with atomics (fold race) and dispatch (event waits).
- The host wake latency on rf. There is a systematic 33 us median GPU start delay at one buffer boundary, 6.5% of the rf step, plus a 23 us tail on gbsa. The dispatch lane owns the wait, but nobody has characterized this boundary. dispatch should take it with the poll change.
- VkFFT grid sizes (58 us each way on apoa1pme, about 6.5%) are only queued as a sweep. pme owns it. Treat it as low priority: the brief estimates 1 to 2%.
- Single-precision integration and constraints are about 30 to 55 us per pme step by census (with about 5 us of overhead per kernel). They are small, and I would not staff them.
- Process gap, which is the biggest one: the lease is a FIFO queue 15 or more deep, and ab.sh re-queues per round and test. At about 21:00Z almost no candidate has any timing on 6df2b8bcb. The lead should give lease slots in the order of section 2 and tell lanes to bundle all their A/B arms into one 20-minute slot.

4. Conflicts and merge order

- NB_TILES (nonbonded) and NB_BATCH (nblist) are the same line in MetalNonbondedUtilities.cpp. One owner, nblist, and one commit.
- nosort (pme) and computeRange (nblist) overlap in saved time, and both touch the Sort path (CommonCalcNonbondedForce.cpp and MetalSort). Land computeRange first, then nosort, and measure nosort on top.
- Force words (atomics) and the TGF, TGP and TGA variants (nonbonded) edit the same atomicAdd write sites in nonbonded.metal (lines ~221-231, 262-268, 435-496). Settle the nonbonded variants first (I expect most to be dropped), then rebase words.
- Force words and PME float atomics: the words measurement is meaningless until 71a602b43 is in the base, because the lo probe counted spreading.
- Force words and concurrent dispatch and the PME stream: the fold is a plain read then zero. It needs a barrier after the last words writer and before the next nonbonded. It also needs the PME stream off, or a separate buffer folded after queueWait.
- PME float atomics and concurrent dispatch create new hazards: gridSpreadCharge writes pmeGrid1, then VkFFT reads it, and on LJPME clearBuffer(pmeGrid1) runs right after gridInterpolateForce. copyShortList (nblist) turns a blit, which was a hard encoder boundary, into a compute dispatch that needs a barrier to sortBoxData.
- Plugins 40b2c41a2 (df64 sin and cos) and mixed c2 (df64 operator changes) both edit df64.metal. That is a textual conflict, and RPMD's mixed trig accuracy should be rechecked after c2. The plugins rewrite pass (createModule, `#define double float`) and the pme lab MSL 4.1 switch both touch module compilation. Keep 4.1 out.
- Plugins' hippoNonbonded.cc depends on the nonbonded forceArgs layout and the nblist tile formats. Any change there needs TestMetalHippoNonbondedForce.
- Mixed c1 edits common integrationUtilities.cc, which OpenCL, the comparison baseline, also compiles.

4. nblist tiles rule

5. Red flags

- Env-knob candidates gate the base path. Tiles, TGF and TGP, PME_LAB_TILED, DISPATCH_MODE and NB_SORT2 are all chosen by getenv. forces.py and ctest run without the variable set, so they test unchanged code and pass trivially. Every candidate must be compiled in as the default before gating.
- The forces gate only sees a fresh neighbor list. It does one evaluation from a fresh Context, so it never sees a stale list. A padding change (NB_PAD) or a rebuild-trigger change that misses interactions between rebuilds would run faster with wrong physics and pass. Any rebuild-policy change needs forces against Reference after 50 to 100 MD steps.
- Constraints are invisible to the gate. Mixed c1 (float SETTLE vel), c2 (sloppy add), c3 (warm start, "normally one pass") and c4 (Newton SETTLE) can all get faster by converging less. A c3 or c4 bug that exits early would be a pure speed "win" with broken constraints. Nothing counts until drift.py and constraints.py pass on both chips.
- The OpenCL denominator can move. c1 changes the SHAKE convergence test in the common kernel that OpenCL single compiles. Ratios must use OpenCL from ultra-base, or the change must be gated to Metal.
- Concurrent auto mode can race quietly. A missed hazard that corrupts a few low-magnitude contributions can stay inside the third-digit rel|dF| tolerance, races are probabilistic, and ctest with the env knob unset tests serial. Only a bitwise diff against serial Metal on the fixed-point systems (rf, gbsa, apoa1rf) proves it.
- Force words carries in words 3 and 4 never fire on gate systems (sums above about 8.4e6). Also, if the PME stream is ever enabled, reciprocal forces can be lost or arrive one step late depending on timing.
- TGP and TGF would break HIPPO. The benchmark systems have no HIPPO, so a per-lane gate passes, and the break first shows in ultra/integrated.
- `#define double float` in every single-precision module means future plugin kernels that ask for double silently get float instead of failing to compile.
- DISPATCH_MODE=none left in the code gives 85% force errors or a GPU page fault if anyone sets it.
- The PME timing screens used DeterministicForces toggles. That is legitimate, but the shipped default on M3 and later is now nondeterministic, and no TestMetal test sets DeterministicForces. Add one repeatability check.
