# Review of the native Metal design study

The research lane reviewed research/2026-09-25-metal-native-design.md on 2026-09-24 at about 21:32Z. It checked the study against labs 008, 011 and 024, the OpenCL submission path and the experiment 028 lane logs. Hostnames are scrubbed.

Design review of research/2026-09-25-metal-native-design.md, checked against labs 008, 011, 024, the OpenCL submission path and the lane logs as of about 21:32Z. Short version: probe 1b as written can't see the overlap it's looking for, dispatch has already built probe 1c but it's being judged against the wrong baseline, and x-resident's rf number is too high. GBSA and the fused integrator hold up. The API check comes next, then the GBSA prototype draft.

PME as its own pass (roadmap 2, probe 1b)
1. On base, P and the list chain sit in different command buffers. prepareInteractions commits X through downloadCountEvent->enqueue() (MetalNonbondedUtilities.cpp:424; MetalEvent::enqueue is encodeSignalEvent plus commit). The PME chain is encoded later, into Y. So findBlocks, sortShortList2 and computeRange, the windows the study counts on, can only be reached across a buffer boundary. There Apple promises start order and nothing more: "The GPU starts the command buffer after it starts any command buffers that are ahead of it" (commit() doc). Fix: build the probe on dispatch's cand/onebuf cfc9547f3 or cand/poll 17fc67494, which already put the list chain and the force kernels in one buffer.
2. The overlap evidence comes from counters mode, and the profiler's split mode undercuts it for plain encoders. One encoder per dispatch with samples: apoa1pme 0.960, cellulose 0.940, stmv 0.944. The same split without samples: apoa1pme 1.003, GPU busy 1776 against 1760 us (profiler.md:70-82). The profiler's untested guess is that the driver merges plain consecutive compute encoders, and splitPass() makes exactly that kind of encoder. My inference: the counters speedup shows up only on PME tests, and findBlocks' raw time is 2x its attributed time on those same tests (profiler.md:24). That fits the PME chain at the head of Y running beside findBlocks at the tail of X when neither encoder is merged. So the lever may be real even if splitPass() alone doesn't trigger it. Add arm B-s: B plus a stage-boundary sample attachment on P's encoder only (GpuProf's hook, 2 samples, never resolved). If B is flat and B-s wins, the design needs a boundary the driver can't merge.
3. The timestamp pass can't judge this. Sample attachments are the variable that changes scheduling, so the pass will show overlap whether or not production gets it. Decide on host-clock B/A. The kill clause "P starts only after D ends" can't fire either, because P is encoded ahead of D. The real no-overlap signal is that the end times of D and findBlocks don't move.
4. The 10 to 25 us overhead estimate is too high, which helps the design. Split mode prices a plain encoder boundary at 4.0% of gbsa over about 20 boundaries, about 0.6 us each, and at nothing on pme (0.998). Lab 008 gives +0.06 us GPU and +0.17 us host per boundary (encoder_per_kernel 1.942/0.284 against one_encoder_serial 1.881/0.112). The study's 8 us per boundary is counters sampling. The real cost is about 1 us of boundaries plus the fold (the 1.9 us dispatch floor plus 3.7 MB on apoa1). 1.03 stays a fair kill bar.
5. Ticket 41058 no longer carries PLAIN or NOWRITE. pme.md:119 and :129 list base, lab, stream, tiled41, ti1024 and b256m5. So item b's no-build kill test for the int tile only has its tiled41 half. Either add the two arms to pme's next ticket or decide on tiled41 alone.
6. Lab 011 agrees with the study's PME inputs: VkFFT at parity on the Ultra (Metal 0.067 against OpenCL 0.068 ms forward, 0.064 both inverse) and the float spread at 0.169 against 0.412 ms fixed point, 2.44x. Two small misquotes: the HEAD-NOTE says gather is "8 to 30x" slower, and the agreement is 0.54 ppm, not 0.3.

Wait-free step (roadmap 4, probe 1c)
7. It's already built: dispatch's probe/wait 99aa5dbd5, DISPATCH_WAIT=none, queued in screen5 with base, one and poll on gbsa and rf.
8. The study's mechanism is stale. The 40 us "blocked waiter" was waitUntilCompleted, which base already replaced with the event wait (lab 024 README:424-528; the X to Y gap fell to 0.4 us). The profiler now splits the remaining gap. The host commits after the GPU has run dry for only 1.4 us/step on gbsa and 3.5 on rf. The rest is commit-to-start latency, about 12 us per buffer on gbsa and 21 on rf (profiler.md:46). Dispatch measured the host busy for about 47 us of a gbsa step (dispatch.md:26), so the host isn't the critical path. That makes the gap a per-buffer cost, and onebuf already drops one buffer per step.
9. So judge none against poll, not against base. Keep phase A only if none beats poll by 3% or more on gbsa or rf, and apply the "gap falls less than 8 us" kill line to poll's gap. My prediction (inference): none, poll and one land within noise of each other, all about one buffer's latency ahead of base. If screen5 shows that, phase A's 36 MB bound and CAS spill buy nothing. Phase B (indirect rebuild skipping) would then move to nblist with the gated chain. The capacity math itself checks out: :257 starts at 20 x numAtomBlocks, and :471-474 caps at totalTiles.
10. OpenCL submission path: OpenCL pays the same per-step count sync. It does a non-blocking read into pinned memory, then downloadCountEvent.wait() and updateNeighborListSize() (OpenCLNonbondedUtilities.cpp:363, :394-395). In lab 008, Metal's serial dispatch already costs less GPU time than OpenCL batched (1.881 against 2.270 us), and step-sync replay is faster (0.567 against 0.651 ms). So the orchestration designs chase costs internal to Metal, and the OpenCL denominator doesn't move.

x-resident tiles (roadmap 1, probe 1d)
11. The premise is verified: tiles go to SIMD groups strided by totalWarps, and force is zeroed per tile.
12. apoa1rf holds, a bit lower. At 21:23Z, 1-atomic ran at 1.654, while force words, one returning atomic per add, got only 1.114 (atomics.md). So the cost is the returning atomic in each emulated add. x-resident removes whole adds, which is the right cut. The proportional math: the plain-RMW pool is 348 us per eval (0.818 to 0.470 ms at 19:55Z), nonbonded's share is 60 to 80%, and adds fall 38%. That gives 79 to 106 us, or 1.07 to 1.10x on the 1144.5 us step. The top of the study's range, 1.20, needs savings that grow faster than linear.
13. rf is overstated, and its pool is unstable across runs: 1-atomic was 1.192 at 19:55Z and 1.019 at 21:23Z. Even at the high number (plain 1.187, equal to 1-atomic, about 46 us per eval), a 29% cut of nonbonded's share is 8 to 11 us. That's about 1.02x, under the keep bar. At the 21:23Z number it's under 1%.
14. If nonbonded's tiles4 lands (gbsa 1.055, rf 1.168, pme 1.088 at 21:18Z), four SIMD groups share each findBlocks row (findInteractingBlocks.metal:337, :385), and each flushes its own tiles. So same-x runs get shorter on rf, pme and dhfr (inference from the code). Screen x-resident on top of tiles4, and don't carry tonight's small-system number forward.

GBSA (roadmap 5, probe 1e, mine)
15. It holds. SQRT is precise::sqrt (common.metal:107), OpenCL uses native_sqrt when its accuracy test passes (OpenCLContext.cpp:391), and RECIP is fast::divide. Both GB kernels launch as 720x64 under the executeKernel cap (MetalContext.cpp:589, with the cap defined at :158 and :172). The RSQRT(r2) is dead code, so the live cut is one precise sqrt plus 3 fast divides, replaced by 1 fast rsqrt. gbsa's atomics probe is 1.051 to 1.056, so the kernel isn't atomic-bound.
16. Two adjustments. Nonbonded's screen found threadgroup staging of j data neutral on the Ultra (tgp and tgpa 0.987 to 1.003), so don't expect much from stage B's register rotation. And the profiler's tbpc24 probe (profiler.md:10) already has data coming for launch shape (c), so the "under 5%, narrow to (c)" branch needs no new build.

Fused LangevinMiddle (roadmap 6)
17. It holds. benchmark.py sends nothing to CCMA (verified, lab 022 README:18-19). The gbsa counters census puts Part1-3 plus the 2 SHAKE kernels at 31 us per step (dispatch.md:30). At lab 008's 1.88 us floor, going from 5 dispatches to 1 saves 7.5 us before any memory savings, so 8 to 10 us on gbsa single is fair. The same census has the CM motion remover's two dispatches at 12 us, about 4% of gbsa, so the fold-in extension is worth scoping early.

Moot or minor
18. The accumulate class (dispatch design 3) is moot: you stopped the concurrent encoder at 21:30Z (auto screened gbsa 0.919, dispatch.md:26 and :31). For the record, Apple's resource-synchronization page does exempt atomic-type accesses from synchronization, so the correctness idea was sound.
