Thanks @exxrxxe, good to see the same design land in the same place independently. Using Claude Fable 5.1, I've since built a full Metal platform (metal-cpp, a thin ComputeContext under Common Compute, Common kernels unchanged) and run it on an M2, an M3 Pro and an M3 Ultra. What got it to OpenCL's speed:

- Primitive args go through `setBytes`, which copies the value at encode time, so CCMA's per-iteration `setArg(i)` needs no per-launch copy. Arrays go through `setBuffer` and are read at execution.
- VkFFT encodes into the open encoder (`params.commandEncoder`), so FFTs need no commit. Upload and download are `finish()` plus `memcpy` on `contents()`. `copyTo` is a blit in the same buffer.
- Commits only where the host reads back, plus one after forces so the GPU runs while the integrator encodes: 1.01 command buffers per step with NoCutoff, 2.01 with PME, asserted in a test. CCMA reads its convergence flag every 4th iteration.
- Safe math with precise functions, then a startup probe (same idea as OpenCL's `determineNativeAccuracy`) moves rsqrt, recip, exp and log to `fast::` only when the max relative error is under 1e-6. sqrt stays precise. Blanket fast math was out: fast asin matches OpenCL bit for bit on only 43% of inputs. Fast recip alone was +26% on dhfr-implicit.

Results on the FAHBench work units, host wall clock:
- Metal single vs OpenCL single: 1.01 to 1.04x on dhfr-implicit and nav, 1.20 to 1.28x on dhfr PME. That dhfr WU is the only system that sends constraints to CCMA (3,072 of them: every bond and H-X-H angle outside water); whether CCMA is the cause isn't isolated yet.
- Mixed precision through df64 (float-float), which Apple's OpenCL can't run: 2.8 to 11x the CPU platform.
- Force error vs Reference: 1.21e-6 on dhfr, 1.75e-6 on nav, same on all three chips. OpenCL is 1.80e-6 to 1.82e-6 on nav.
- ctest: 109/110 on M2, 107/110 on M3 Pro, 108/110 on M3 Ultra. The one deterministic failure, testLargeForces, also fails with unmodified OpenCL on both M3 chips and passes on the M2. Filed as #5434.

Agreed on SIMD. Standalone, `simd_ballot` findBlocks runs 2.3 to 3.1x faster (GPU timestamps). It isn't in the platform yet; that's next.
