# Saturate float to fixed point conversions in OpenCL

Fixes #5434.

In OpenCL C, converting an out-of-range float to `long` is implementation-defined. On Apple M3 GPUs it wraps modulo 2^64. On the M2 it saturates, as it does on NVIDIA. The minimizer detects overflowing forces by looking for fixed point values beyond ±2^62. On M3, the forces in `testLargeForces` became exactly 0, the gradient norm was 0, and L-BFGS stopped at its starting point.

- `realToFixedPoint` in common.cl uses `convert_long_sat`.
- The kernels that converted a force to fixed point with a plain cast now go through `realToFixedPoint`:
  - customCppForce and pythonForce
  - atmforce and customCentroidBond
  - customCVForce's generated kernel in CommonKernels.cpp
  - `getConstraintEnergyForces` in minimize.cc
  - RPMD's `contractForces`
  - OpenCL's `reduceReal4Buffer`
- atmforce, customCentroidBond and customCVForce already hold fixed point values, and RPMD's `FORCE_SCALE` includes the 2^32 factor. So these divide by 2^32 first, which is exact.
- amoebaVdwForce1 rescales fixed point forces by a factor of at most 1. It can only go out of range if the force has already saturated, so I left it.
- CUDA and HIP keep their own `realToFixedPoint`, unchanged.

One behavior change: in mixed precision, the minimizer's constraint forces and the RPMD contraction are now rounded to float before the conversion. This happens on all three platforms, because `realToFixedPoint` takes `real`. Every other force is already converted this way. If you'd rather keep those two in full precision, I can leave them as plain casts.

Tested on an M3 Ultra, macOS 27.2: the OpenCL tests pass 66 of 66 with this change, and 65 of 66 on master. The one failure on master is `TestOpenCLLocalEnergyMinimizerSingle`. The stochastic `TestOpenCLMonteCarloAnisotropicBarostatSingle` failed once with this change and passed on three reruns. Apple's OpenCL has no double precision, so the double and mixed tests weren't built. I haven't tested on CUDA or HIP hardware.
